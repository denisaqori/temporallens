| D9  | Epoch selection is the **smoothed validation peak**: a trailing moving average (10–20 epochs) of validation macro-F1, selecting the epoch that maximizes the smoothed curve; fold metrics are the smoothed values there, and the refit trains to the median of those epochs. Amends D6/D7 — no metric ever comes from a single epoch (the prior work's instinct), but selection follows the validation peak rather than an arbitrary fixed budget (which reports wherever the run ended, overfit or not). Smoothing window is a protocol constant, not a tuning knob                   | Claude (synthesis of the group's prior method and the raw-best-epoch alternative)                                                                           | Denisa     | 2026-07-29 | Accepted — spec §5.2                                                                                                                                                          |
| D10 | The refit's test score is **cross-checked one-sidedly against the eight fold-model test scores**. The refit trains on 32 subjects vs. each fold's 28, and training-subject count is the binding constraint in subject-independent EMG, so scoring **above** the fold mean is expected and is not a flag; scoring **below** it is the defect signal (epoch budget transferring badly from 28 to 32, class weights from the wrong split, or a bug in the refit path). Refit-minus-fold-mean is recorded as a result in its own right — the marginal value of four more training subjects | Claude (check; corrected to one-sided by Denisa, who noted the extra subjects should raise the refit); uses the prior work's fold-models-on-test evaluation | Denisa     | 2026-07-29 | Accepted — spec §5.3                                                                                                                                                          |
| D11 | Class imbalance is handled **in the loss** (`class_weighted_cross_entropy`), **never by resampling**, and the test set is **never balanced**. Departs from the group's prior work, which oversamples within subject: windows overlap 75% at stride 100, so duplicating one puts near-identical copies in a batch — the window-correlation hazard §3.2/§3.3 warn about, reintroduced through the back door                                                                                                                                                                              | Claude (raised the divergence and the reason)                                                                                                               | Denisa     | 2026-07-29 | Accepted — spec §3.6; applied to all 8 classification configs, including the three foundation ones that declared no loss at all                                               |
| D12 | Labels and repetition indices are read from **`restimulus`** and **`rerepetition`**, never `stimulus`/`repetition`. `stimulus` records what the acquisition software prompted, which the DB2 descriptor states does not match what the subject did ("human reaction times"); the authors realigned the boundaries offline and shipped the corrected column. Loading `stimulus` mislabels every movement onset by the reaction time and fails silently. Relatedly, **no baseline/rest subtraction** — rest is one of the 18 classes, so removing the rest level would erase what defines it | Claude (found it in the descriptor, at Denisa's request to read it) | Denisa | 2026-07-30 | Accepted — spec §3.1; assert the label column on load |
| D13 | **Repetition policy:** the subject is the split unit, so all 6 of a subject's repetitions follow that subject; repetitions are never divided across train/validation/test. Departs from the dataset's own benchmark (test = repetitions 2 and 5, within subject), which measures session generalization rather than subject generalization — so our numbers are not comparable to their ~75% DB2 figure. For subject calibration, **calibration draws from repetitions {1, 4} and evaluation from {2, 3, 5, 6}**: disjoint per leakage rule 3, and spanning early and late because the descriptor measured significant amplitude drift across repetitions in 12.5% of DB2 subjects | Claude (derived from the descriptor's drift analysis) | Denisa | 2026-07-30 | Accepted — spec §3.2 and generative-arm G3. Unblocks the split manifest |
